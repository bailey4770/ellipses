## Elliptical orbits in three dimensions

In the [previous post](/ellipses/2026/05/10/ellipses-and-elliptical-orbits.html)
we explored elliptical orbits in two dimensios. But to understand eclipses, we
need to add the third dimension.

This figure shows an observer (the black sphere), a star (the yellow sphere) and
an elliptical orbit around it (blue curve). The blue plane is the XY plane, or
the sky plane. It shows the sky, as viewed from the observer, centred around the
star. In the previous post, the ellipses and elliptical orbits were flat on this
plane. However, with the addition of the third dimension, we introduce another
aspect of elliptical orbits: the inclination $i$.

## Geometry of eclipses

Consider a planet of radius $R_P$ and mass $M_P$ orbitting a star of radius
$R_\*$ and mass $M_*$. Let's define the ratio of planet to star radius:

$$
k = \frac{R_P}{R_*} \tag{1}
$$

the origin at the star, with the X-Y planes on the celestial sphere and the +Z
